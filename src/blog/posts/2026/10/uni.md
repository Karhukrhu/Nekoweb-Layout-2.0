I've noticed that many people in the community are at the "uni point" of their lifes. This inevitably brings back my memories of the time I spent at the university. Those where the second most horrible times of my life, actually. Of course there were good parts and bad parts, but heck the bad parts overweigh the good ones so bad!

So where and what did I study and why? I was very abitious during upper secondary school (equivalent for highschool) and I realized I would never get money out of what I really wanted to do, art. So I decided to go study business, where the real money talks like really. Really. Most of the times I had been doing very well at school. I was one of the best at elementary school and pretty good one at secondary school as well. So I had gained some ambition during those years and had this naive idea that I could do anything I ever wanted and be good at it. 

Unfortunately I got just what I wanted: an admission to the probably most popular and prized university in my country. To study business. Of course it wasn't a piece of cake for me, but I had people who backed me up financially and got me to the best admission test course there was. And I studied pretty hard and it paid well. I got to the university I wanted. At that point everything seemed to be like dream becoming true. Like there's nothing to stop me. Like now I get the best business education there is in this country! Nothing could go wrong!

Except that everything did. Well, not everything, but most of the things. One of the best things that happened was that I realised I wasn't the best at everything like I had naively thought before. I always got the best grades before, but now I was very much a mediocre. And after I noticed studies were actually pretty hard, I started getting below mediocre. And at some point of the first year of my uni studies, I realised I wanted to drop the studies 'cause I wasn't interested in the subject anymore. But poeple had invested in me financially, I had to go on. 

This was the everyday struggle from now on: I needed to show my relatives I could do this and graduate. And that this really was what I wanted even though it wasn't. From now on it was just grinding my teeth till I collapsed. There was a health check for every new student and that's when I got diagnosed with depression first time. I was like 19? 20? At the point when the health check was conducted I was already seeking for a proper tree to hang myself during every.. fucking... busride to the uni from my home.

So how about my uni friends? Didn't I get any help from them? Any support? No, because I had no friends at uni. I had zero friends. The most important reason for that was that I absolutely secluded myself from everyone, because I was thinking I was saving them from a stupid asshole who would eventually kill themselves anyway. So I did zero initiative towards getting any friends. I was thinking I was saving everyones time and effort.

This of course led to the fact I never attended any uni events that there was. Great opportunities to have fun with people, get connected with the community, have potential friends. I was a weird hermit that hoped to get through as little contact with other students and teachers as possible. This of course only worsened my situation at the uni and eventually led to a drop out.

So the base facts about me not continuing past bachelor was 1. no motivation 2. worsening depression. I tried really hard though. I pushed throgh ALL the master courses and even attended an exchange period in a foreign country that I enjoyed a lot! It was the master's thesis that really got me. I wasn't able to write it. I feel like I had nonexistent support for that. My supervisor was very distant and wasn't particularly supportive. And I had no friends to turn to get support.

I wasn't going to give up easy though. I tried 3 fucking years to write it and always just applied for extended study time. I just wasn't making it. At some point I realised I'm not gonna make it at all. During the first time of applying an extension, my student psychologist said I should give up already. That suggestion lived withing me for three freaking years. Until I realized I should face the disappointment in myself. I promised myself I would get a driving licence for motorcycle when I graduate, but that's just never going to happen.

It was both a disappointment and a relief. I didn't have to do it anymore. I didn't have to stuggle to do something I had zero motivation. Yes, I'm left without a bike license, but that's okay. Yes, I'm left without a high paying job, but that's okay. I've come past those. What I was left with, was tormenting depression and an inability to speak out my problems to anyone. I still, after 5 years of quitting, am not able to speak to the people that supported me financially to get to the uni that I had to quit. Not even my parents. It's a disapointment not to talk about. That's just the culture running in my family.
